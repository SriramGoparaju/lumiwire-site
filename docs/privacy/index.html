Lumiwire Privacy Policy

Last updated 11 November 2026

Lumiwire is a connect-the-wires puzzle game. This policy explains what it collects, why, who handles it, and what you can do about it.

The test versions of Lumiwire available now show no ads and sell nothing. The sections on advertising and purchases describe how those will work once they arrive, so that this policy does not have to change when they do.

  • You can play all of Lumiwire without giving us your name, email address or phone number. There is no sign-up.
  • The app makes an anonymous account for itself, so your progress can be saved to the cloud and your results can appear on the leaderboards.
  • On the leaderboards, other players see a display name (a random one until you choose your own) and your result. Nothing else you do is public.
  • We do not sell your data and we do not share it with data brokers. Ads come from Google AdMob, which may use your device's advertising ID; where the law requires a consent form, ads and analytics wait for your answer. Crash reports are on from the start and carry no account identifier.
  • Settings → Delete account deletes your account and everything stored with it, at once.

Who is responsible

Lumiwire is published by Sriram Goparaju, in India. For any question about this policy or your data, write to sriramgoparaju99@gmail.com.

What we collect, and why

An anonymous account, made automatically

The first time you open Lumiwire, it signs in anonymously through Firebase Authentication, a Google service. This creates a random account identifier. No name, email address or phone number is attached to it, and we cannot use it to find out who you are. It exists so your progress can be saved to the cloud and your results can be placed on the leaderboards.

Because nothing identifies the account, we cannot look it up for you. If the app's data is lost, for example when you uninstall it, the account cannot be recovered, and on a new phone the app starts a new one.

Your display name

A display name is shown next to your results on the leaderboards. Until you choose one, it is a random pair of words such as "Copper Fox". You can change it at any time in Settings → Display name, up to 16 characters. Please don't use your full real name or anything else that identifies you. Changing it also updates your rows on today's leaderboards.

Your progress and settings

Lumiwire saves your progress on your phone and, when you are online, to your account in Google Cloud Firestore: the stars you earned on each level and where you are in the Journey, the Daily puzzles you solved and your times, your Blitz results and bests, your display name, your settings (display mode, sound, music, haptics, timer), and a list of the leaderboard rows your account has written, so that they can be deleted with it. Once coins and the Shop arrive, the save also holds your coin balance, the items you own and use, your achievements, and which purchases your account holds.

The cloud copy can be read and written only by your account.

Leaderboard results

When you solve the Daily puzzle, or finish a Blitz round with a new best, the result is sent to that leaderboard: your time for the Daily; your score and time for Blitz today; your weekly total for Blitz week. Each row carries your display name and is stored under your account identifier. A result is sent only when it beats your own earlier result on that board. If you are offline it waits on your phone and goes up when you reconnect.

Anyone playing Lumiwire can see the leaderboards: the display name and the result. The account identifier is part of each row's address in the database, so other players' apps can technically see it too; it says nothing about who you are.

Crash reports

Firebase Crashlytics records crashes, and some errors the app recovers from, so they can be found and fixed. A report holds the device model, the operating system and app version, the state of the app and the place in the code that failed, and a random identifier for this installation of the app. Crash reporting is on from the first launch. It is not used for advertising, and it carries no account identifier and no display name.

How the game is played

Firebase Analytics counts how Lumiwire is played: that a level was started or finished (with the level, the stars, the number of strokes, the seconds taken, the hints used and the obstacles on the board), that a Daily or a Blitz round was finished, that a chapter opened, that an account was deleted; and, once ads and the Shop arrive, that an ad was watched, a hint used, a purchase made, a cosmetic chosen, and how you answered the consent question. Analytics also records basic information about the device (model, operating system, app version, language, and a country estimated from the IP address) under its own random identifier for this installation. It is not given your account identifier or your display name.

Analytics collection is switched off when the app is installed. It is switched on only after the consent step described under Advertising: where Google's consent form is required, only if you agree; elsewhere, once the app has confirmed that no form is needed where you are. Until that consent step exists in the app, Analytics stays off.

Settings we can change remotely

The app asks Firebase Remote Config for settings that can change without an app update, such as the length of a Blitz round or the addresses of these web pages. The request carries a random identifier for this installation of the app, the app version, and your device's language and region settings. Nothing about your play is sent with it.

Advertising

Lumiwire shows ads through Google AdMob. There are two kinds: a short full-screen Ad break between levels (after every third Journey level from level 6, or after a Blitz round, and never in the middle of a board), and rewarded videos that play only when you choose to watch one, for a hint, extra coins, extra Blitz time and the like. There are no banner ads. Remove Ads, a one-time purchase, ends the Ad breaks; the videos you choose stay available.

When it shows an ad, Google's advertising SDK collects, by Google's own description, your device's advertising ID, its IP address (which may be used to estimate your general location), information about the device and the app, and how you interact with ads, such as taps and video views. Google uses this to choose, show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, under Google's privacy policy. Lumiwire asks AdMob only for ads suitable for teenagers and adults, and tells it that the app is not directed at children.

Asking you first

Where the law gives you a choice (the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, and the US states with their own privacy laws), Google's consent or opt-out form appears after the short introduction and before any ad is requested; nothing is fetched from AdMob until you have answered. If you decline, Google may still show limited or non-personalised ads, within the choices you made. Where the form applies, Settings shows Privacy options, which reopens it, so you can change your answer at any time.

On iPhone and iPad, Apple's question about whether the app may track you across other companies' apps and websites comes once, after the consent form. Saying no changes only how relevant the ads are; the game is exactly the same. You can change the answer in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's privacy settings.

Purchases

Purchases are made through Google Play or the Apple App Store, which handle the payment; we never see or store your card or bank details. RevenueCat, a service that manages in-app purchases, receives the store's receipt for each purchase together with an anonymous identifier for your copy of the app, so it can confirm what you bought and let you restore it. Your save records which purchases your account holds, and which coin-pack purchases have already been credited, so that coins are never credited twice.

What we don't collect

Lumiwire does not collect your name (apart from a display name you choose), email address, phone number, contacts, photos, files or messages, and it does not ask for your location. It has no chat and no way to message other players. Google's ad and analytics services may estimate a general location from your IP address, as described above.

Where it is stored

The cloud save and the leaderboards are stored in Google Cloud Firestore in the United States. Everything the app sends travels encrypted.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and its cloud save: until you delete the account.
  • Leaderboard rows: kept as the record of each day and week until you delete your account (the app shows only today's and this week's boards).
  • Crash reports: 90 days, on Crashlytics' own schedule.
  • Analytics events: on Google's schedule, at most fourteen months.
  • On your phone: until you delete the account, reset your progress, clear the app's data or uninstall it.
  • Purchase records: kept by Google Play, the App Store and RevenueCat for their accounting and tax records.

Your choices

  • Choose your display name in Settings → Display name, or keep the random one.
  • Answer the ad consent form as you wish, and change your mind with Privacy options in Settings, where the form applies.
  • Say no to tracking. On iPhone and iPad, in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. On Android, reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings. The game works exactly the same either way.
  • Delete your account. Settings → Delete account deletes your cloud save, your rows on every leaderboard and the account itself, then everything on the phone, at once. The delete-account page has the details, including what stays and why.
  • Ask us. For a copy of your data, or anything else, write to sriramgoparaju99@gmail.com. Because accounts are anonymous, we may need details from your phone (your display name and the days you played) to find your data, and we cannot act on data we cannot connect to you.

If you are in the UK, the EU or another region with similar law, you also have the right to access and correct your data, to have it deleted, to object to how we process it, and to complain to your data protection authority.

Children

Lumiwire is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child has given us data, write to sriramgoparaju99@gmail.com and we will remove it.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we will update the date at the top and say so in the app before the change takes effect.
